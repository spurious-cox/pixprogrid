=============================================================================
 PixProGrid — setup note
=============================================================================

Pixelmator Pro won't let some tools work with the grid switched off. This
little app doesn't switch it off — it makes it invisible. One switch: on for
clean screenshots, off to put your own settings back.

While it's on, the grid is set to one line every 100% with a single
subdivision, so the only gridlines land on the canvas edges, in light gray.
It also turns off the transparency checkerboard, so transparent areas grab as
flat light gray instead of a checker pattern.


-----------------------------------------------------------------------------
 BEFORE YOU START
-----------------------------------------------------------------------------

* An Apple-silicon Mac (M1 or later). It will NOT run on Intel.
* macOS 13 or later.
* Pixelmator Pro, running in English. The app finds Pixelmator's settings by
  reading the labels in its Settings window, so a Pixelmator running in
  another language won't work.


-----------------------------------------------------------------------------
 INSTALLING
-----------------------------------------------------------------------------

1. Drag PixProGrid.app into your Applications folder.

2. The first time you open it, macOS will refuse and say it can't check the
   app for malicious software.

   That's expected. It's signed with a personal certificate rather than a
   paid Apple developer one, and it isn't notarised — macOS treats anything
   in that position the same way, whoever wrote it.

   To get past it: open System Settings > Privacy & Security, scroll down to
   the message about PixProGrid being blocked, and click "Open Anyway".

   If you'd rather do it in Terminal, this does the same thing:

       xattr -dr com.apple.quarantine /Applications/PixProGrid.app

3. Open PixProGrid. The panel will say it's waiting for Accessibility access.

   It changes Pixelmator's settings by driving Pixelmator's own Settings
   window, which macOS won't let any app do without permission. Without this
   the app can do nothing at all.

   Go to System Settings > Privacy & Security > Accessibility, add or switch
   on PixProGrid, then quit and reopen it.

   It should now say "Ready."


-----------------------------------------------------------------------------
 USING IT
-----------------------------------------------------------------------------

Have Pixelmator Pro open, then flip the switch on.

Give it about ten seconds. Pixelmator comes to the front, its Settings window
opens and closes, and the status line under the switch tells you when it's
finished. Wait for that line before you judge whether it worked — it is not
instant.

Flip the switch off, or click Dismiss, to put your own settings back. Both
check that every setting actually returned before reporting success. Quitting
restores them too.

Ctrl-Option-Cmd-G flips the switch from whatever app you're in — but only
while PixProGrid is already running. It won't launch it.


-----------------------------------------------------------------------------
 IF SOMETHING GOES WRONG
-----------------------------------------------------------------------------

Your settings are saved to disk BEFORE anything is changed. If the app is
force-quit or the Mac restarts while the screenshot settings are applied, the
next launch notices and puts your settings back on its own.

If the status line reports that a setting wouldn't take, the switch stays on
and your settings are still applied — try again, or click Dismiss. Nothing is
lost either way.

The app has no Dock icon and no menu bar. The panel is the app: closing it or
clicking Dismiss quits it.
